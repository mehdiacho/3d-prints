SIZESEvery BA to BP size is a proposal, now modelled as v1. The first print is the fit test: how the bore grips the plug, and how the hook sits in the trough.
HOLDHA to HG are worked out, not tested: PETG at 2000 MPa, friction 0.3, the arm bending over 24. Read them as "about", and against each other.
V7The trough is measured at three points; the curve between is assumed (sheet 01, V4). If it is steeper near the ridge the hook holds better, if flatter, worse. BP and HA to HE move with it.
HHThe cable's own bend force is not measured (no scale to hand): TBD. V1 is exported with both the 2.4 and the 3.0 arm, so the print decides instead.
V1The model adds three fit allowances not drawn here: 0.3 left between the halves so the ties can clamp, the bore opened to 11.7 over the bump, and the windows cut right through.
HEATThe vent blows hot air 4 from the shell. PETG softens near 80 °C and creeps under steady load well below that. Exhaust temperature there: TBD.
KEYED NOTES
1THE HOOK IS A SPRING CATCH, NOT A LATCH. The trough behind the bar is a smooth slope with no ledge, so the lip can only ride up it. It holds by the arm's spring, about 26 N.
2THE SHELL IS NOT THE WEAK PART. At 10 N on the mouth it carries about 4 MPa against roughly 40. The clip gives way long before the shell would.
3SIDEWAYS TOWARDS THE VENT IS THE WEAK DIRECTION. The plate cannot be wider there without covering the vent. Point the elbow so the cable's bend pushes towards the HDMI side, or down.
4A THICKER ARM TRADES HOLD FOR EFFORT. 3.0 instead of 2.4 doubles every holding figure and doubles the drag when sliding on. Both go in the fit test.
5IT LETS GO BEFORE ANYTHING BREAKS. A hard snag on the cable pulls the hook out at about 2.6 kg. That is kinder to the jack than a brace that cannot release.
6EACH HALF PRINTS ON ITS SPLIT FACE. No supports, and the layers run along the arm, the direction it bends in. Printed standing up, the arm would snap along a layer.
