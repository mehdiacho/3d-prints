1ONE BAY PER TRAY, ALL THE SAME. Print as many as you have trays. Each is 200 × 114 and fits the bed in one piece.
2THE TRAY STANDS ON TWO FLOOR RAILS, ONE UNDER EACH ROW OF CELLS. It slides in from the open front until its back cells meet the stop. Its ends overhang the bay a little.
3A BOSS AND A MAGNET AT EACH CORNER. The boss drops into the socket under the bay above and lines them up; 5 × 3 mm neodymium discs hold them. Glue a stack together instead if you like.
4IN A CHEST FREEZER, WORK FROM THE TOP. Lift the top bay off, slide its tray out, put the bay back. Two stacks fit side by side in 300 of width.
5FILL AT THE TAP. This sheet alone: fill each tray to 300 at the sink, slide it in. Rigid trays carry fine. Sheet 03 (splitter) and sheet 05 (cascade) add pouring in place; the base on sheet 05 levels any of them.
6PETG IN THE FREEZER. PLA turns brittle in the cold. About 62 g a bay at solid fill; four bays about 250 g, so buy a fresh spool before printing all of them.

1ONE POUR, FOUR EQUAL SHARES. A tray only spills over its lip once it is full to the brim, so tray-to-tray overflow would force 400 into every tray. Instead the pour lands in a trough and splits four ways.
2EQUAL HOLES AT THE SAME HEIGHT PASS THE SAME WATER. Four 6 mm holes sit together in a sump. Each feeds its own tube to one bay. The holes set the split; the tubes are wider and never run full.
3NOTHING MAY HOLD WATER BETWEEN FILLS. The floor slopes to the sump and the tubes fall all the way, so everything drains dry and nothing freezes over a hole.
4RAISE THE BACK A LITTLE. The spouts drop into the back cells. Tilted back, water must climb to the front cells and the back cells freeze over their walls; tilted a touch forward (a shim under the back), it runs downhill and every cube snaps apart. The split between trays barely changes with tilt: within about 5 ml at 0.5°. Try it in the fill simulation.
5MARK A BOTTLE AT 1200, AND DON'T RUSH IT. 300 for each tray in use; push a plug (SO) into the hole of each bay you are not using: using bays 1, 2 and 4, plug hole 3 and pour 900. Under about 60 ml a second the trough never fills; tipped right over, a bottle can run over its rim.
6SPOUTS ALTERNATE SIDES. Each bay has a port in both side walls; use the left on bays 1 and 3, the right on 2 and 4, so the tubes never cross.
7DRILL THE HOLES TO SIZE. Print them at 5.6 and open all four with the same 6 mm drill bit. A printed hole can come out 0.1 or 0.2 off, and 0.2 on 6 mm passes 7 % more water; drilled, they match.

1SHELVED (11 Oct). Left out of the fill system: water does not spread from cell to cell, and the soft tray spills off its edge as easily as into the next cell. Kept here for later.
2MEHDI'S IDEA FOR LATER. A plate that fills every gap under the cups, then fill over the top so it freezes as one block; the soft tray lets it break apart.
3THE CUPS SET THE BASE, NOT THE OUTLINE. E, F and G give a cell spacing of 38.5 along and 43.5 across; the plate's ribs follow it.
4TWO PRINTS, GLUED RIB TO RIB. Split across the middle; each half is 160 × 185 and fits the 220 bed.

1EVERY TRAY FILLS TO THE BRIM, THEN SPILLS INTO THE NEXT. Pour 1600 into the top tray. It fills, spills over its lip, and the catch pan under its bay sends the water to the tray below, and so on down. Each tray ends at 400 (AO) with its cubes joined by a skin of ice: for 300 and cubes that snap apart, use the splitter on sheet 03.
2ADD-ON PIECES; THE BAYS STAY THE SAME. A pan sits on the posts of the bay below and hangs into the room above that bay's tray; the bay above sits on the pan's bosses. Lift the pans out and it is the plain stack of sheet 02. Three pans for four trays: the top tray is poured into, the bottom one spills into the base.
3THE PAN CATCHES ALL ROUND. A full tray spills wherever its lip is lowest, so the pan reaches past both ends of the tray and across to the walls, and its floor falls 3 to one drain.
4SKIP A BAY BY LEAVING ITS TRAY OUT. The pan above drips through the empty bay onto the next pan, and on down.
5THE BASE IS FOR ANY FILL. Under the bottom bay: four printed screw feet level the whole stack (a turn is 3), and its basin holds about 400, so an overpour from the splitter or the cascade never reaches the freezer floor.
6TRIED BEFORE. Canadian patent application CA 2029293 (1990) fills a stack of three trays this way: each spills into the next through an overflow at one end, with a container at the bottom for the surplus and marks on the jug for one, two or three trays.
7PANS PUT 2.3 BETWEEN THE MAGNETS. With a pan between two bays their magnets no longer touch: the bosses still line them up, but they hold less. Pause the print to drop magnets in if you would rather not glue them.
