How it is worked out

Trough. 120 × 114 outside, floor sloping 4 down to a 36 mm sump, rim 32.4 over the holes; it holds about 390 ml. Four 6 mm holes 18 apart in the sump. Each passes 0.62 × area × √(2 g h), where h is the water over that hole: the tilt lowers some holes and raises others.

Tubes. 8 mm inside, wider than the 6 mm holes, so they never run full and do not change the split. Each delivers into one end cell of its tray.

Trays. Each tray is twelve cells. 300 ml brings every cell to 1 under the walls between cells (X); 400 to the brim (AO). Water crosses a wall between cells through the gaps at the ends of its ridge (AB, 2 × 3.45 wide) once it is over the wall top, and over the whole ridge 2 higher (W); the middle wall has no ridge. Each crossing is a weir: 0.41 × width × √(2 g) × H^1.5.

Tilt moves every cell floor, wall top and hole by its distance from the centre. A cell more than 0.5 over a wall at the end is counted as joined to its neighbour: that film freezes into a bridge.

One inlet per tray. Water fills the cell it lands in up to the wall top before it crosses, so the cells nearest the spout end at the wall top and the far end cells get what is left. Pouring 300 ml into one cell gives cubes that snap apart, with the far end cubes a little smaller.

Resting state. After the trough runs dry, the last slow creep over the walls is sped up so the table shows where the water comes to rest; where it ends up does not depend on how fast it gets there.

Not modelled: the tube's travel time, splashing, water clinging to the trough and tubes (a few ml each), and the cells' taper (each cell has one area below the walls and one above).
